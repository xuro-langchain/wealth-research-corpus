## View at a glance

> **Demonstration-corpus status:** The underlying note is explicitly synthetic; its figures are invented and it is not research, a recommendation, or investment advice. [source notice](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L1-L3)

**Edition:** MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12, published 10 December 2025 by Multi-Asset Research; it applies to positions taken on or after 1 January 2026 and is a global view. The research recommendation is a **10–20% strategic allocation** to private markets for eligible clients that have a genuine ten-year horizon. The note was an initiation (no prior view). [V.1](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L16-L18); [publication terms](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L7-L14)

This is a research view, not a determination that a client may receive private-markets strategies. **Eligibility is an external regulatory and compliance constraint, not a conclusion of this research.** The note limits its view to clients already eligible and directs the eligibility question to qualified-client and accredited-investor rules and firm guidance. [V.2](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L20-L22)

For the global balanced mandate, the committee's standing default is 10% private markets for eligible clients; ineligible clients have that sleeve reallocated pro rata across liquid sleeves. The research range should therefore not be read as authority to override the committee allocation policy. [allocation guide B.2](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L7-L16)

## Positioning within the sleeve

The recommended implementation is weighted toward **secondaries** and **private credit**, and away from **primary buyout**. [summary and V.1](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L12-L18)

- **Secondaries — overweight within the sleeve.** Although discounts to NAV have narrowed, the desk judges them still wide relative to the ten-year median for buyout portfolios and calls secondaries the most attractive entry point in the complex. [V.3](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L24-L26)
- **Private credit — favored, with underwriting vigilance.** The desk sees spreads over broadly syndicated loans as adequate compensation for illiquidity, while monitoring weaker covenant quality at the larger end of the market as private credit competes with syndicated issuance. [V.4](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L28-L30)
- **Primary buyout — underweight, not excluded.** Entry multiples are above the desk's estimate of fair value and exits have not normalized; the prescribed response is an underweight within the allocation rather than avoiding the asset class. [V.5](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L32-L34)

## Preconditions and commitment control

The horizon and liquidity tests are conditions of the view, not return assumptions. A client needs a genuine ten-year horizon. [V.1](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L16-L18) Before a commitment, underwriting must be against the client's spending requirement rather than expected allocation return, and **unfunded commitments must not exceed two years of liquid-portfolio spending**. [V.6](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L36-L38)

Operationally, the control sequence is:

1. Compliance determines and documents eligibility before any strategy is offered; portfolio-manager discretion cannot substitute for that determination. [eligibility guide P.1](repo://guidelines/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md#L7-L10)
2. Eligibility remains necessary but insufficient: suitability and the V.6 liquidity constraint must both be met before a commitment. [eligibility guide P.6](repo://guidelines/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md#L31-L35)
3. Pace commitments rather than attempting ordinary rebalancing. Private-markets drift resulting from a denominator effect is not a breach and is not traded; drift caused by over-commitment is a pacing failure that must be escalated under the allocation guide's D.4 process. [allocation guide B.6](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L31-L35)

The distinction matters because private markets has no ordinary tolerance band: the exposure cannot be traded in the same way as liquid sleeves. The principal failure modes identified by the research are an extended closure of the exit environment (lengthening duration in an unrebalanced asset class) and drawdown-driven denominator effects. [allocation guide B.3](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L17-L19); [V.7](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L40-L42)

## Governance and use boundaries

Use the research note to set the private-markets mix and to assess whether the horizon and liquidity conditions support a commitment. Use the allocation guide for the mandate-level strategic default and pacing/escalation rules, and use the eligibility guide for the separate legal/compliance gate. The allocation policy explicitly makes the V.6 unfunded-commitment liquidity constraint binding, and it suspends a band that rests on a superseded or withdrawn research note pending committee review. [allocation guide B.1](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L7-L11); [allocation guide B.6](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L31-L35)

Eligibility documentation must record the pathway, supporting evidence, decision maker, and date; an undocumented determination is treated as no determination in audit. That documentation obligation does not convert eligibility into an investment recommendation. [eligibility guide P.5](repo://guidelines/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md#L27-L29); [V.2](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L20-L22)
