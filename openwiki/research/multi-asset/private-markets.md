## Scope and edition

This page records **MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS, edition 2025-12**, published by Multi-Asset Research on 2025-12-10 and applicable to positions taken on or after 2026-01-01. It is a global research view, not client-specific investment advice or binding allocation guidance. [Source: framework introduction](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L1-L3)

## Strategic view

For an **eligible client with a genuine ten-year horizon**, the desk supports a **10–20% strategic private-markets allocation**. Within that allocation, it prefers secondaries and private credit and recommends an underweight to primary buyout at current entry multiples. The horizon, range, and relative tilts are one research conclusion; they should not be applied to a client who cannot bear the stated horizon. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.1](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L5-L7)

### Preferred exposure: secondaries

The desk considers secondaries the most attractive entry point in the complex. Its stated basis is that buyout-portfolio secondary discounts to net asset value have narrowed but remain wider than their ten-year median. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.3](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L13-L15)

### Preferred exposure: private credit

The desk judges the spread over broadly syndicated loans to remain adequate compensation for illiquidity. Covenant quality is the key watch item: it has deteriorated at the larger end of the market, where private credit competes directly with syndicated issuance. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.4](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L17-L19)

### Relative underweight: primary buyout

The view is to underweight—not eliminate—primary buyout within a private-markets allocation. The desk cites entry multiples above its estimate of fair value and an exit environment that has not normalised. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.5](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L21-L23)

## Liquidity is the binding implementation limit

The research framework says to underwrite private markets against the client's **spending requirement**, rather than expected return, and recommends that unfunded commitments not exceed **two years of liquid-portfolio spending**. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.6](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L25-L27)

The allocation guidance makes that recommendation a binding firm limit: do not allow unfunded commitments above two years of liquid-portfolio spending. It also implements the 10% private-markets sleeve in a global balanced mandate through commitment pacing rather than ordinary rebalancing. A denominator-effect increase above target is not a tradable band breach; an increase caused by over-commitment is a pacing failure requiring escalation. These are implementation controls, not changes to the research view. [Global Multi-Asset Allocation Bands B.2 and B.6](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L13-L15) [Global Multi-Asset Allocation Bands B.6](repo://guidelines/allocation/gl-multi-asset-bands.md#L31-L35)

## Eligibility is a regulatory constraint, not a research conclusion

The research view applies **only after** the client is eligible. Qualified-purchaser and accredited-investor thresholds are regulatory matters; the research desk neither determines eligibility nor uses its allocation view to alter it. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.2](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L9-L11)

The Private Markets Eligibility Determination Guide implements the regulatory gate: no strategy may be offered until eligibility has been determined and documented, and portfolio-manager discretion cannot substitute for that determination. Eligibility remains necessary but insufficient; suitability and the V.6 liquidity constraint must also be satisfied before commitment. [Private Markets Eligibility Determination Guide P.1 and P.6](repo://guidelines/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md#L7-L9) [Private Markets Eligibility Determination Guide P.6](repo://guidelines/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md#L31-L35)

For the controlling thresholds, effective-date treatment, and recordkeeping, use the [SEC qualified-purchaser regulatory overlay](/openwiki/regulatory/sec-qualified-purchaser.md) and the [private-markets eligibility and suitability guidance](/openwiki/guidance/suitability/private-markets-eligibility.md). Do not infer an eligibility result from the 10–20% research range, the ten-year horizon, or an approval decision.

## Risks to monitor

The note identifies two portfolio-level failure modes: a prolonged closure of the exit environment can extend duration in an asset class that cannot be rebalanced, and a drawdown can create denominator effects that mechanically push the allocation through an untradable band. These risks explain the need for horizon underwriting, commitment pacing, and the spending-based liquidity limit. [MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS 2025-12 V.7](repo://research/MA/GL/PRIVATE-MARKETS/2025-12.md#L29-L31)

## Decision sequence

1. Establish and document regulatory eligibility under the separate compliance process; if it is not met, the research view does not apply.
2. For an eligible client, test whether the client has a genuine ten-year horizon and whether the proposed 10–20% strategic exposure fits the research view.
3. Underwrite unfunded commitments against liquid-portfolio spending and keep them at or below the binding two-year limit.
4. Express exposure through pacing, with a relative preference for secondaries and private credit and an underweight to primary buyout; handle over-commitment as an escalation, not a rebalance.

This sequence preserves the ownership boundary: research informs positioning after the regulatory gate, while compliance determines eligibility and allocation guidance constrains implementation.
