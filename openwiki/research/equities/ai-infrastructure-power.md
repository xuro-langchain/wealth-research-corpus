## Scope and stance

**EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER, edition 2026-01** is a global equity-research view, published on 2026-01-22 and applicable to positions taken on or after 2026-02-01. It is explicitly not limited to one market. The desk is overweight (1) regulated utilities whose service territories have data-centre load growth and (2) electrical-equipment manufacturers with transformer and switchgear capacity. It expresses no directional view on compute vendors in this edition. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L1-L7)

This is a **power-sector and electrical-infrastructure** thesis, not a semiconductor-equipment recommendation. Its investment mechanism is that economic rent follows the binding constraint: when deployment is restricted by grid access, grid equipment, or generation adequacy, the beneficiaries are expected to sit in those constrained parts of the infrastructure chain. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L9-L13)

## Constraint migration: accelerator supply to electricity

The note characterizes accelerator supply as the binding AI-infrastructure constraint through 2024, but expects electrical infrastructure to bind through 2026. The relevant gates are:

1. a project’s position and completion path in the **interconnection queue**;
2. **transformer lead times**; and
3. in some jurisdictions, **generation adequacy**.

The causal chain is therefore: AI/data-centre load growth requires a connection and physical power equipment; a delayed queue outcome, unavailable transformer, or inadequate generation delays energization; the delay shifts scarcity value toward utilities and electrical-equipment suppliers rather than establishing a directional compute-vendor view. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L7-L13)

### Interconnection as the entry gate

Interconnection is not treated as a simple administrative step. The note reports that median study completion in the largest US market operator exceeded 40 months. It also says that queue reform reduced speculative-project counts but did not materially shorten timelines for remaining projects. Thus, removing speculative applications is not, in this view, sufficient evidence that viable data-centre or fab projects can connect faster. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L15-L17)

### Equipment as the physical bottleneck

For the highest-voltage classes, large power-transformer lead times are stated as three to four years. The desk’s model says capacity additions announced through 2025 do not close that gap before 2029. Switchgear capacity is included alongside transformer capacity in the stated electrical-equipment overweight, so the thesis is about the delivery chain needed to turn an interconnection outcome into usable power—not transformers in isolation. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L23-L25) [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L5-L7)

## Limited modification to semiconductor capacity

The power view modifies—but does not replace—the semiconductor-capacity path. **EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER 2026-01 P.4** states that grid-interconnection timelines increasingly gate leading-edge-fab siting in several jurisdictions. The asserted effect is to cap the **rate** at which new capacity comes online, rather than the **level of eventual demand**. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L19-L21)

This proposition is directionally corroborated by **EQ-US-SEMI-CAPEX 2026-02 S.4**, which calls grid-interconnection timelines, rather than tooling availability, the increasingly binding siting gate for leading-edge fabs and applies the same rate-versus-level distinction. The semiconductor note remains a separate US sector view: it reduces US semiconductor capital equipment to neutral from overweight on valuation and cycle position, while saying that the power constraint is covered by this global power note. [Source](repo://research/EQ/US/SEMI-CAPEX/2026-02.md#L5-L7) [Source](repo://research/EQ/US/SEMI-CAPEX/2026-02.md#L19-L21)

In practical terms, this cross-note relationship means electrical constraints can stretch the timing and shape of capacity additions even where the long-run demand premise remains intact. It should not be read as a claim that power limitations erase eventual semiconductor demand or as a reversal of the semiconductor note’s independent neutral-weight decision.

## Risks and monitoring boundaries

The load-growth and scarcity mechanism can fail or weaken if:

- compute energy efficiency improves materially per unit of useful work;
- regulation reallocates interconnection capacity away from data-centre load on affordability grounds (the note says this has been proposed in three jurisdictions); or
- AI capital formation slows, removing the assumed load-growth premise.

These are thesis risks, not a claim that any named policy outcome will occur. Monitor queue completion time for viable projects, transformer and switchgear delivery capacity, generation adequacy where load is proposed, compute-energy efficiency, and changes to interconnection allocation policy. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L27-L29)
