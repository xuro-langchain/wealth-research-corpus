# Global AI Infrastructure: Power as the Binding Constraint

## Scope, edition, and positioning

This page records **EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER edition 2026-01**, published 2026-01-22 and applicable to positions taken on or after 2026-02-01. It is a Global Equity Research view, expressly global rather than a single-market view. The note is synthetic demonstration material, not research, a recommendation, or investment advice. [Edition, applicability, and scope](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L1-L14)

The desk is **overweight** (1) regulated utilities whose service territories have data-centre load growth and (2) electrical-equipment manufacturers with transformer and switchgear capacity. The view does **not** take a directional position on compute vendors. Its thesis is that economic rent follows the binding constraint: whereas accelerator supply was the limiting factor through 2024, the desk expects electrical constraints to bind through 2026. [Summary view and constraint premise](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L16-L24)

## What is constrained

The relevant electrical constraints are interconnection-queue position, transformer lead times, and, in some jurisdictions, generation adequacy. The note reports median interconnection-study completion beyond 40 months in the largest US market operator; queue reform is said to have reduced speculative-project counts without materially shortening the remaining projects' timelines. This supports a global view but should not be read as a claim that every jurisdiction has the same bottleneck. [Constraint components](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L20-L28)

Electrical-equipment exposure is specifically tied to transformers and switchgear. For the highest-voltage large power transformers, the desk puts lead times at three to four years and models capacity additions announced through 2025 as insufficient to close the gap before 2029. [Transformer and switchgear capacity](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L34-L36)

## Semiconductor-capacity interaction

This is not a separate compute-vendor thesis. Grid-interconnection timelines increasingly gate leading-edge fab siting in several jurisdictions, so the constraint caps the **rate** at which new semiconductor capacity can come online, rather than the eventual level of demand.

**EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER 2026-01 P.4 modifies EQ-US-SEMI-CAPEX 2026-02 S.4**: the global note frames electrical interconnection as a modifier to the semiconductor-capital-equipment cycle's capacity-addition path, while the US semiconductor note likewise treats grid interconnection—not tooling availability—as the siting gate and the effect as a cap on addition rate rather than level. [AI infrastructure provision](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L30-L32) [Semiconductor-capex provision](repo://internal_research/EQ/US/SEMI-CAPEX/2026-02.md#L30-L32)

See also [Semiconductor capital expenditure](/openwiki/research/equities/semiconductor-capex.md) for the related US cycle view. The current 2026-02 note is neutral on US semiconductor capital equipment, having reduced the prior overweight on valuation and cycle position rather than deteriorating fundamentals. [Current US summary](repo://internal_research/EQ/US/SEMI-CAPEX/2026-02.md#L16-L18)

## Risks and monitoring

The stated risks are: materially better compute energy efficiency per unit of useful work; regulatory allocation of interconnection capacity away from data-centre load on affordability grounds (proposed in three jurisdictions); and a slowdown in AI capital formation. The last would remove the data-centre load-growth premise rather than merely soften the preferred exposures. [Stated risks](repo://internal_research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L38-L40)

Accordingly, review the view against changes in useful-work energy efficiency, interconnection-allocation policy, AI-capital-formation conditions, and the availability/timing of transformer and switchgear capacity. These are monitoring implications of the note's stated premise and risks, not additional recommendations.
