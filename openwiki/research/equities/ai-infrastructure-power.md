## Current market view

**EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER, edition 2026-01** was published by Global Equity Research on 2026-01-22 and applies to positions taken on or after 2026-02-01. It is a global (not single-market) research view. Its positioning is **overweight** regulated utilities with data-centre load growth in their service territories and electrical-equipment manufacturers with transformer and switchgear capacity; it has no directional compute-vendor view. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L5-L18)

The note is an initiation with no prior view. Its central premise is that economic rent follows the binding constraint, making it a power-sector and electrical-infrastructure thesis rather than a compute-vendor call. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L7-L12) [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L20-L24)

## Moved constraint and transmission mechanism

The desk describes accelerator supply as the binding constraint through 2024, but expects the constraint through 2026 to be electrical: interconnection-queue position, transformer lead times, and, in several jurisdictions, generation adequacy. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L20-L24)

The mechanism has three linked gates:

1. **Interconnection:** a project needs a viable queue position and completed study process before it can connect.
2. **Electrical equipment:** a connection requires physical equipment, including high-voltage transformers and switchgear.
3. **Adequate generation:** in affected jurisdictions, available generation must be sufficient for the new load.

Accordingly, delay at any gate can slow energization of AI and data-centre infrastructure, while scarcity is expected to accrue to the utilities and equipment suppliers identified in the current view. This is the note's constraint-migration rationale, not a directional conclusion about compute vendors. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L16-L24)

### Interconnection is a timing gate

Median interconnection-study completion in the largest US market operator has extended beyond 40 months. The note says queue reform has reduced speculative-project counts without materially shortening the timeline for projects that remain; fewer speculative applications therefore do not by themselves demonstrate faster connection for viable projects. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L26-L28)

### Transformer and switchgear capacity is a delivery gate

For the highest-voltage classes, large power-transformer lead times are stated as three to four years. The desk's modelling says capacity additions announced through 2025 do not close the gap before 2029. The positioning explicitly includes manufacturers with both transformer and switchgear capacity, so the expression covers the electrical-equipment delivery chain rather than transformers alone. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L16-L18) [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L34-L36)

## Bounded modification of semiconductor-capacity timing

**EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER 2026-01 P.4 modifies EQ-US-SEMI-CAPEX 2026-02 S.4:** grid-interconnection timelines increasingly gate leading-edge-fab siting in several jurisdictions. The documented consequence is a cap on the **rate** at which new capacity comes online, not on the **level** of eventual demand. This relationship is deliberately narrow: it changes the expected timing and shape of capacity additions. It does not replace the semiconductor note's cycle view, establish a semiconductor allocation, or turn the power view into an allocation instruction. ## Risks and monitoring

The desk identifies three conditions that could weaken or remove the load-growth/scarcity premise:

- a material improvement in compute energy efficiency per unit of useful work;
- regulatory allocation of interconnection capacity away from data-centre load on affordability grounds—an approach proposed in three jurisdictions; and
- a slowdown in AI capital formation, which would remove the load-growth premise. [Source](repo://research/EQ/GL/AI-INFRA-POWER/2026-01.md#L38-L40)

Operational monitoring should therefore focus on the observable inputs to the thesis: completion time for viable interconnection projects, transformer and switchgear delivery capacity, generation adequacy where applicable, compute-energy efficiency, interconnection-allocation policy, and AI-capital-formation momentum. These indicators test the stated electrical gates and risks; they do not themselves create a new positioning statement.
