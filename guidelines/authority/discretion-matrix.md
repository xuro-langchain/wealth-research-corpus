# Portfolio Manager Discretion and Escalation Matrix

Internal guidance establishing what a portfolio manager may do without approval, and which conditions require escalation, across all discretionary mandates. This is not a research note and not investment advice to any client. Mandate-specific guidance may impose tighter limits but may never grant broader discretion than this document.

Last revised 2026-02-11 by the Investment Policy Committee.

## D.1 Authority Tiers

Portfolio managers hold base discretion. Senior portfolio managers hold expanded discretion and may clear one escalation condition per account. Committee approval is required for anything exceeding senior discretion, for any account carrying more than one escalation condition, and for any exception to a stated band.

Where a mandate guide states a tighter band than this document, the mandate guide governs. Taxable fixed income bands are at A.6 of the taxable fixed income guide and global multi-asset bands are at B.3 of the bands guide.

## D.2 Position Size

Portfolio managers may take a single-issuer position up to three percent of account market value. Senior portfolio managers may take up to five percent. Above five percent requires committee approval and a written concentration rationale, and the position is thereafter subject to the concentrated position guide at C.2.

## D.3 Conditions Requiring Approval

The following require approval regardless of size.

Any weight outside its stated tolerance band, other than a band breach arising from a suspension, which is governed by D.4 instead.

Any position in a sector a governing research note places on underweight, where the mandate guide makes that preference binding as a limit. The taxable fixed income guide does so at A.4.

Any hedge of a concentrated position, per C.4.

Any extension of municipal duration beyond the band stated at A.4 of the taxable fixed income guide. This may not be granted on a portfolio-wide basis.

Any private markets commitment, which additionally requires the eligibility determination at P.1 before approval is sought.

## D.4 Escalation on a Superseded Note

Where a portfolio manager finds that a research note a mandate guide cites has been superseded or withdrawn, the weight derived from that note is suspended under the mandate guide and the manager must escalate to the committee rather than re-deriving the weight.

The escalation records the superseded note, the replacement if one exists, every weight the guide derived from it, and the accounts affected. A manager may not carry the prior weight forward pending review, and may not adopt the replacement note's recommendation directly: adopting a view into a binding weight is a committee act, not a portfolio manager act.

This is the condition most often handled incorrectly, because the prior weight is usually still defensible and carrying it forward feels conservative. It is not conservative; it is an unapproved position.

## D.5 Conditions That May Not Be Cleared

Some conditions may not be approved at any level.

Trading employer securities on firm discretion during a blackout period, per C.5.

Any private markets commitment for a client who is not eligible under P.1. Eligibility is a regulatory determination and approval is not a substitute for it.

Any position that would breach a stated regulatory requirement. Regulatory requirements are not investment discretion, and an approval cannot create an exception to them.

## D.6 Documentation Standard

Every cleared escalation records the condition, the authority level that cleared it, the specific facts relied on, and the date. An escalation cleared without a recorded basis is treated as an unapproved position in audit and is charged back to the clearing manager's file review.
