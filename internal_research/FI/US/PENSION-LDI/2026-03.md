## L.1 Summary View

The desk moves long-dated investment grade credit to overweight from neutral. The recommendation is a demand call: corporate defined benefit plans are expected to remain persistent buyers of long corporate paper through 2028, and that flow is large relative to net issuance at the long end.

## L.2 Why the Demand Persists

Corporate plans in surplus de-risk by extending duration and rotating out of return-seeking assets into long credit. Aggregate funded status has held above one hundred percent for eight quarters.

We are leaning on the funding rules here, and it is worth being explicit about why. Sponsors lock in because a surplus is worth protecting: contribution requirements plus the discount-rate basis for measuring liabilities give them the reason. Change those rules so a sponsor can release or re-risk a surplus and the flow at L.1 can go into reverse, which the overweight would not survive.

## L.3 Supply

Net long-dated corporate issuance has run below the L.2 demand for six quarters. We do not expect issuers to close the gap while front-end funding stays cheaper.

## L.4 Credit Quality

Single-A and better at the long end. We are being paid for the flow, not for credit risk, so there is no case for reaching down in quality to express it.

## L.5 Relationship to the Broader Credit View

This sits alongside the index-level underweight at G.1 of FI-US-IG-SPREADS 2025-09 without contradicting it. That note prices the index on valuation. This one is about where the flow goes in a single maturity bucket.

## L.6 Risks to the View

A change to pension funding rules permitting surplus release, altering the discount-rate basis, or cutting contribution requirements. This is the one that takes out L.2 and the recommendation with it.

A sharp increase in long-dated issuance. Spreads widening far enough that the flow stops being price-insensitive.

## L.7 Review Trigger

Reviewed at twelve months, or sooner on any announced change to pension funding or discount-rate rules.
