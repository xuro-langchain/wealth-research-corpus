Published 2025-06-12 by US Fixed Income Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2025-07-01. This note states the desk's view on United States municipal credit and is the basis of record for municipal weightings in the taxable-account allocation guidance.

## M.1 Summary View

The desk moves United States municipal credit to overweight from neutral, with the overweight concentrated in the high-yield and lower-investment-grade tiers of the private activity bond segment. The recommendation rests on an after-tax spread advantage that is structural rather than cyclical, and on a supply picture that we expect to stay constrained through 2027.

The overweight is expressed as a two to four percentage point increase in municipal weight within the taxable fixed income sleeve, funded from investment grade corporate credit.

## M.2 The After-Tax Case

Private activity paper trades inside comparable corporates pre-tax across most of the rating spectrum. The pickup shows up after tax, and only for top-bracket holders.

Section 57(a)(5)(A) makes interest on post-1986 specified private activity bonds an item of tax preference, so it enters AMTI. We expect that treatment to hold.

The threshold is what does the work. At the amounts in force today the exemption does not reach zero until $978,750 for a single filer and $1,800,700 for joint filers, which leaves most of our top-bracket book outside the AMT altogether. Those clients never trigger the preference and take the coupon free of federal tax. We put the pickup at 90-110bp.

We are leaning hard on that. Credit fundamentals on their own support a neutral to modest overweight and no more; the extra two points are the after-tax pickup. Cut the phase-out threshold far enough to pull a meaningful share of top-bracket holders into the AMT and the pickup compresses toward nothing, taking M.1 with it. Our exposure here is the threshold. The section 57 treatment itself we regard as stable.

## M.3 Credit Fundamentals

State and local tax receipts have grown eleven quarters running. Rainy day balances across the fifty states sit near multi-decade highs against expenditure. Default experience in the rated municipal universe is an order of magnitude below comparably rated corporates, and recoveries on the defaults that do happen are better.

On its own this gets us to neutral, maybe a point over. The rest of M.1 rests on M.2.

## M.4 Supply

Issuance has run below the ten-year average for six quarters. Refunding volume is suppressed by the rate environment and by the 2017 Act's removal of advance refunding. We see net supply staying negative through 2027 in private activity specifically, where the volume cap has not moved in nominal terms since its indexation base was set.

This technical stands on its own and does not depend on M.2.

## M.5 Sector Preferences

Within the private activity segment the desk prefers, in order: non-profit hospital systems with demonstrated pricing power and three or more years of positive operating margin; private higher education with endowment coverage above four times annual operating expense; and airport special facility paper at large hub airports with signatory carrier agreements running beyond 2035.

The first two are predominantly issued as qualified 501(c)(3) bonds under section 145. The third predominantly is not. The desk notes the distinction here because section 57(a)(5)(C)(ii) excepts qualified 501(c)(3) bonds from the preference treatment altogether, so the sector preferences carry different exposure to the risk at M.7.

The desk is underweight standalone senior living, single-asset student housing, and industrial development paper secured by a single corporate obligor, in all cases regardless of rating.

## M.6 Duration and Curve

Express the overweight in the eight to fifteen year part of the curve. The desk does not recommend extending municipal duration beyond fifteen years at current ratios, and does not recommend the front end, where the after-tax advantage described in M.2 is smallest in absolute terms and least durable.

## M.7 Risks to the View

In order of concern:

A cut to the AMT phase-out threshold, by indexation methodology or by statute. This is the one that takes out M.2 and the recommendation with it. A change to the preference treatment would do the same, but we see it as far less likely. See M.2.

A move in the top marginal federal rate, which scales the pickup either way.

A reversal in supply, most plausibly a statutory increase in the private activity volume cap.

Credit deterioration in the preferred sectors. Containable at the position size we are recommending.

## M.8 Review Trigger

Reviewed at twelve months, or sooner on any announced change to the federal tax treatment of municipal interest. A change to what M.2 describes goes to immediate review and re-issue; it does not wait for the cycle.
