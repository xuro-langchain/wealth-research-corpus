## D.1 Summary View

The desk recommends a modest long duration position of plus zero point four years against benchmark, expressed in the five to ten year part of the curve, and recommends against expressing duration at the long end.

## D.2 Policy Path

The Committee raised the target range to 3-3/4 to 4 percent at its September meeting, framing the increase as supporting a timelier return to the 2 percent goal. See FED Statement 2026-09-16 at F.1 and F.2.

The desk reads that statement narrowly, and F.5 of it is the reason: it is a decision and not a path. The desk's central case is one further increase over the coming twelve months against a market-implied one and a half. The half-step difference is the whole of the duration recommendation and should be understood as a modest deviation, not a conviction call.

## D.3 Why Not the Long End

Term premium has re-established itself at a level the desk regards as fair to cheap, but supply at the long end remains heavy and the desk sees no catalyst for compression inside twelve months. A long-end duration position would express a term premium view the desk does not hold.

## D.4 Curve

The desk expects continued steepening between two and ten years and recommends expressing the duration position with a steepening bias rather than as a parallel extension.

## D.5 Risks

An inflation print sequence that turns the single increase the desk assumes into a sequence. The July meeting carried three dissents preferring an increase, per F.6, so the hawkish tail is not hypothetical. A fiscal event that increases long-end supply beyond current projections. A disorderly repricing of term premium, which would hurt the position at D.1 despite its front-loaded expression.
