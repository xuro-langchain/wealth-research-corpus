# IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 — Tax Year 2026 Inflation Adjustments: AMT Exemption and Phase-Out

Issued 2025-10. Applies to taxable years beginning in 2026. Trimmed digest of a real published release, retained in this corpus for the provisions that bear on municipal positioning. Full text: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf

## N.1 Scope of This Digest

Revenue Procedure 2025-32 sets the inflation-adjusted amounts for more than sixty Code provisions for taxable years beginning in 2026. Only the alternative minimum tax items are reproduced here. The remainder of the revenue procedure is outside this corpus.

## N.2 AMT Exemption Amount and Phase-Out Threshold, Tax Year 2026

For taxable years beginning in 2026 the alternative minimum tax exemption amount is $90,100 for an unmarried individual and $140,200 for married individuals filing a joint return. The exemption begins to phase out at $500,000 of alternative minimum taxable income for an unmarried individual and at $1,000,000 for married individuals filing jointly.

The phase-out thresholds stated above are materially lower than those in force for taxable years beginning in 2025, under which the exemption did not reach zero until $978,750 for an unmarried individual and $1,800,700 for married individuals filing jointly. The reduction follows the reset of the AMT phase-out thresholds enacted in 2025 and is not an inflation effect.

## N.3 Why the Threshold Matters More Than the Exemption

The exemption amount rose with inflation. The phase-out threshold did not — it was reset downward. The consequence is that the population of taxpayers for whom alternative minimum tax is operative widens for taxable years beginning in 2026, notwithstanding a nominally larger exemption.

## N.4 Interaction With Private Activity Bond Interest

Under section 57(a)(5)(A), tax-exempt interest on a specified private activity bond is an item of tax preference and is included in alternative minimum taxable income. Section 57(a)(5)(C)(i) defines a specified private activity bond as a private activity bond issued after 1986 the interest on which is not includible in gross income under section 103.

This revenue procedure does not change that treatment. What it changes is the threshold at which the preference produces an actual liability, per N.2.

## N.5 The Qualified 501(c)(3) Exception Is Unchanged

Section 57(a)(5)(C)(ii) provides that, for purposes of clause (i), the term private activity bond does not include any qualified 501(c)(3) bond as defined in section 145. Interest on a qualified 501(c)(3) bond is therefore not an item of tax preference and is not drawn into alternative minimum taxable income.

Nothing in this revenue procedure disturbs that exception. The distinction is material to any position taken on the private activity segment: obligations of non-profit hospital systems and private higher education institutions are commonly issued as qualified 501(c)(3) bonds, while airport special facility obligations commonly are not.

## N.6 Other Statutory Exceptions Preserved

Section 57(a)(5)(C)(iii) excepts certain qualified residential rental, qualified mortgage, and qualified veterans' mortgage bonds. Section 57(a)(5)(C)(iv) excepts refundings where the refunded or original bond was issued before 1986-08-08. These exceptions continue to apply in full and are unaffected by the amounts at N.2.

## N.7 Reliance

The amounts at N.2 apply to taxable years beginning in 2026. The revenue procedure cautions that if amendments to the Code were enacted for 2025 or 2026 after 2025-10-09, taxpayers should consult later guidance.
