## M.1 Summary View

The desk moves United States municipal credit to neutral from overweight. The private activity concentration recommended in edition 2025-06 is withdrawn and is replaced by a narrower preference limited to qualified 501(c)(3) obligations.

This supersedes edition 2025-06 in its entirety for positions taken on or after 2026-05-01. Edition 2025-06 remains the basis of record for positions taken while it stood.

## M.2 Why the Prior View Does Not Survive

Edition 2025-06 rested at M.2 on an after-tax advantage available to top-bracket holders who fell outside the alternative minimum tax under the phase-out thresholds then in force, under which the exemption did not reach zero until $978,750 for an unmarried filer and $1,800,700 for a joint filer. That note stated the assumption as load-bearing and stated at M.7 that a reduction in the phase-out threshold would invalidate the recommendation.

IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-41 at N.3 sets the phase-out threshold for taxable years beginning on or after 2026-01-01 at $500,000 for an unmarried filer and $1,000,000 for a joint filer. The exemption amount at N.2 rose, but the threshold at N.3 did not: it is stated as a fixed amount and is expressly not indexed before 2030. The population for whom the alternative minimum tax is operative therefore widens notwithstanding the larger exemption.

On the desk's modelling of the amounts at N.3, the majority of the top-bracket client base that the prior note assumed to be outside the alternative minimum tax falls inside it for taxable years beginning in 2026. The after-tax advantage that justified the overweight compresses to approximately fifteen basis points on the desk's central case, from the ninety to one hundred and ten basis points assumed in edition 2025-06. That is inside transaction costs for the segment.

The overweight therefore does not survive, and the desk withdraws it rather than re-justifying it on the fundamental case at M.3 of the prior edition, which the prior edition itself stated was insufficient to support the position size.

## M.3 What Is Preserved

Section 57(a)(5)(C)(ii) excepts qualified 501(c)(3) bonds as defined in section 145 from the preference treatment altogether, and IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-41 expressly leaves that exception undisturbed at N.5. Interest on those obligations never enters alternative minimum taxable income, so the thresholds at N.3 do not reach them.

Two of the three preferred sectors in edition 2025-06 at M.5 — non-profit hospital systems and private higher education — are predominantly issued as qualified 501(c)(3) obligations. For those sectors the after-tax case is intact and the desk retains a modest preference.

The third preferred sector at M.5, airport special facility paper, is predominantly non-501(c)(3) private activity issuance. That preference is withdrawn in full.

## M.4 Revised Positioning

Municipal credit to neutral weight. Within the municipal allocation the desk prefers qualified 501(c)(3) obligations of non-profit hospital systems and private higher education, on the fundamental criteria stated at M.5 of edition 2025-06, which are unchanged.

The desk recommends no new positions in non-501(c)(3) private activity paper. Existing positions are not recommended for forced sale: the thresholds at N.3 apply to taxable years beginning on or after 2026-01-01, and an orderly reduction will realise better execution than a compressed one.

## M.5 Supply

The supply picture described at M.4 of edition 2025-06 is unchanged and remains a genuine technical support. It was never sufficient on its own to justify the overweight and it is not sufficient now.

## M.6 Duration and Curve

Unchanged from edition 2025-06 at M.6. The eight to fifteen year band remains the desk's preference and the front end remains unattractive.

## M.7 Risks to the View

A statutory restoration of the phase-out thresholds to their pre-2026 levels would re-open the question this note closes. The desk does not regard that as the base case inside twelve months.

A change in the top marginal federal rate continues to scale the after-tax advantage in both directions.

## M.8 Review Trigger

Reviewed on the earlier of its twelve-month anniversary or any announced change to the AMT phase-out thresholds or to the treatment of municipal interest.
