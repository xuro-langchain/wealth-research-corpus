# Concentrated Position Suitability Guide

Internal compliance guidance on holding, hedging, and unwinding concentrated single-security positions in client accounts. This is not a research note and not investment advice to any client.

Last revised 2025-10-27 by Compliance.

## C.1 Definition

A position is concentrated where it exceeds fifteen percent of the client's liquid portfolio market value, or ten percent where the security is the client's employer or an affiliate of it.

## C.2 Standing Requirement

A concentrated position requires a documented suitability determination at acquisition and at each annual review. The determination records the client's stated reason for holding, the tax cost of unwinding, and any restriction that prevents it.

## C.3 What May Not Be Assumed

A client's stated preference to hold is not by itself a suitability determination. The file must record that the concentration risk was explained and that the client's decision was informed.

Low tax basis is a reason to plan an unwind carefully. It is not a reason to omit the determination, and it does not make an unsuitable concentration suitable.

## C.4 Hedging

Hedging a concentrated position requires approval under the discretion matrix at D.3 regardless of size. Collars, prepaid forwards, and exchange funds each carry tax consequences that must be addressed in writing before execution.

## C.5 Employer Securities

Where the security is the client's employer, the file must additionally record any trading window, blackout period, or pre-clearance requirement that applies, and any Rule 10b5-1 plan in force.

A position in employer securities may not be traded on the firm's discretion during a blackout period. This is a prohibition and may not be cleared by approval.

## C.6 Unwind Planning

Every concentrated position carries a written unwind plan, reviewed annually. A plan that has not changed in three consecutive reviews is evidence that it is not being applied and is escalated under D.4.
