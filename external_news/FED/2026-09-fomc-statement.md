Issued 2026-09. Effective on publication.

## F.1 Decision

The Federal Open Market Committee decided today to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent. In support of its dual mandate, the Committee judges that a somewhat more restrictive stance is appropriate at this time.

## F.2 Rationale

Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent objective. The Committee judges that the action taken today supports a timelier return to that objective, and remains firmly committed to returning inflation to 2 percent.

## F.3 Economic Conditions

Recent indicators suggest that economic activity has continued to expand at a solid pace. Domestic spending has held up despite elevated uncertainty. Productivity growth has been strong and capital investment robust. Job gains have kept pace with growth in the labor force and the unemployment rate has changed little in recent months.

## F.4 Balance Sheet

The Committee is continuing its policy of maintaining ample reserves in the banking system. The Committee will continue reducing its holdings of Treasury securities and agency mortgage-backed securities at the pace previously announced. No change to that pace is announced in this statement.

## F.5 Policy Path

In determining the extent of any additional adjustments to the target range, the Committee will take into account the cumulative effects of its actions, the lags with which monetary policy affects economic activity and inflation, and economic and financial developments.

This statement does not describe a path and does not commit the Committee to any sequence of future adjustments. The Committee will assess incoming data and the evolving outlook at each meeting, and is prepared to adjust the stance of policy as appropriate if risks emerge that could impede the attainment of its goals.

## F.6 Votes

Voting for the monetary policy action were all participating members. There were no dissenting votes.

At its meeting of 2026-07-29 the Committee maintained the target range at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent. Three members dissented at that meeting, each preferring to raise the target range by 1/4 percentage point.
