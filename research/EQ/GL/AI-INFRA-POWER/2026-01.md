# Global AI Infrastructure and Power Demand (Note EQ-GL-AI-INFRA-POWER, Edition 2026-01)

Published 2026-01-22 by Global Equity Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2026-02-01. This note takes a global view and is not scoped to a single market.

## P.1 Summary View

The desk is overweight regulated utilities with data centre load growth in their service territory, and overweight electrical equipment manufacturers with transformer and switchgear capacity. The desk holds no directional view on the compute vendors themselves at this edition.

## P.2 The Constraint Has Moved

Through 2024 the binding constraint on AI infrastructure build-out was accelerator supply. Through 2026 the desk expects the binding constraint to be electrical: interconnection queue position, transformer lead times, and in several jurisdictions generation adequacy.

Where a constraint moves, the economic rent moves with it. That is the whole of the investment case in this note.

## P.3 Interconnection Queues

Median interconnection study completion in the largest United States market operator has extended beyond forty months. Queue reform has reduced the count of speculative projects without materially shortening the timeline for the projects that remain.

## P.4 Effect on Semiconductor Capacity

Grid interconnection timelines now gate leading-edge fab siting in several jurisdictions. This caps the rate at which new capacity can be brought online and is a modifier on the semiconductor capital equipment cycle view; it changes the shape of the addition, not the level of eventual demand. See EQ-US-SEMI-CAPEX 2026-02 at S.4.

## P.5 Transformer and Switchgear

Large power transformer lead times stand at three to four years for the highest voltage classes. Capacity additions announced through 2025 do not close the gap before 2029 on the desk's modelling.

## P.6 Risks

A material improvement in compute energy efficiency per unit of useful work. Regulatory allocation of interconnection capacity away from data centre load on affordability grounds, which has been proposed in three jurisdictions. A slowdown in AI capital formation, which the desk does not forecast but which would remove the load growth premise entirely.
