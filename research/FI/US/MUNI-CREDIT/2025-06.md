# US Municipal Credit Outlook (Note FI-US-MUNI-CREDIT, Edition 2025-06)

Published 2025-06-12 by US Fixed Income Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2025-07-01. This note states the desk's view on United States municipal credit and is the basis of record for municipal weightings in the taxable-account allocation guidance.

## M.1 Summary View

The desk moves United States municipal credit to overweight from neutral, with the overweight concentrated in the high-yield and lower-investment-grade tiers of the private activity bond segment. The recommendation rests on an after-tax spread advantage that is structural rather than cyclical, and on a supply picture that we expect to stay constrained through 2027.

The overweight is expressed as a two to four percentage point increase in municipal weight within the taxable fixed income sleeve, funded from investment grade corporate credit.

## M.2 The After-Tax Case

The case for the private activity segment is an after-tax case and not a spread case. On a pre-tax basis private activity paper trades inside comparable corporate credit for most of the rating spectrum. The advantage appears only after tax, and only for holders in the top federal bracket.

Under section 57(a)(5)(A), interest on a specified private activity bond issued after 1986 is an item of tax preference and enters alternative minimum taxable income. That treatment is long-settled and the desk does not expect it to change.

What makes the segment work is the threshold, not the treatment. Under the exemption amounts and phase-out thresholds in force at the date of this note — the exemption reaching zero above $978,750 for an unmarried filer and $1,800,700 for a joint filer — the large majority of our top-bracket client base sits outside the alternative minimum tax entirely. For those clients the preference item is never operative and the interest is received free of federal tax.

**This is the load-bearing assumption of the note.** The overweight is not justified on credit fundamentals alone. If the phase-out threshold is reduced such that a materially larger share of top-bracket holders are drawn into the alternative minimum tax, the after-tax advantage compresses toward zero and the recommendation in M.1 does not survive it. The desk stresses that the threshold, not the preference treatment at section 57(a)(5)(A), is the exposure.

## M.3 Credit Fundamentals

State and local tax receipts have grown for eleven consecutive quarters. Rainy day balances across the fifty states stand near multi-decade highs relative to expenditure. Default experience in the rated municipal universe remains an order of magnitude below comparably rated corporate credit, and the recovery experience on the defaults that do occur remains materially better.

The fundamental picture supports a neutral to modest overweight on its own. It does not support the size of the overweight recommended in M.1, which depends on M.2.

## M.4 Supply

Issuance has run below the ten-year average for six consecutive quarters. Refunding volume has been suppressed by the rate environment and by the 2017 Act's removal of advance refunding. We expect net supply to remain negative through 2027 in the private activity segment specifically, where the volume cap has not been raised in nominal terms since its indexation base was set.

Constrained supply is a genuine technical support and is independent of M.2.

## M.5 Sector Preferences

Within the private activity segment the desk prefers, in order: non-profit hospital systems with demonstrated pricing power and three or more years of positive operating margin; private higher education with endowment coverage above four times annual operating expense; and airport special facility paper at large hub airports with signatory carrier agreements running beyond 2035.

The first two are predominantly issued as qualified 501(c)(3) bonds under section 145. The third predominantly is not. The desk notes the distinction here because section 57(a)(5)(C)(ii) excepts qualified 501(c)(3) bonds from the preference treatment altogether, so the sector preferences carry different exposure to the risk at M.7.

The desk is underweight standalone senior living, single-asset student housing, and industrial development paper secured by a single corporate obligor, in all cases regardless of rating.

## M.6 Duration and Curve

Express the overweight in the eight to fifteen year part of the curve. The desk does not recommend extending municipal duration beyond fifteen years at current ratios, and does not recommend the front end, where the after-tax advantage described in M.2 is smallest in absolute terms and least durable.

## M.7 Risks to the View

The material risks, in the desk's order of concern:

A reduction in the AMT phase-out threshold, whether by indexation methodology or by statute. This is the risk that would invalidate M.2 and with it the recommendation. A change to the preference treatment itself would do the same but is materially less likely. See M.2.

A change in the top marginal federal rate, which scales the after-tax advantage in both directions.

A reversal in the supply picture, most plausibly through a statutory increase in the private activity volume cap.

Idiosyncratic credit deterioration in the preferred sectors, which the desk regards as containable at the recommended position size.

## M.8 Review Trigger

This note is reviewed on the earlier of its twelve-month anniversary or any announced change to the federal tax treatment of municipal interest. A change to the treatment described in M.2 requires immediate review and re-issue rather than scheduled review.
