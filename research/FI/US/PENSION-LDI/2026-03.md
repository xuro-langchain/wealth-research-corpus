## L.1 Summary View

The desk moves long-dated investment grade credit to overweight from neutral. The recommendation is a demand call: corporate defined benefit plans are expected to remain persistent buyers of long corporate paper through 2028, and that flow is large relative to net issuance at the long end.

## L.2 Why the Demand Persists

Corporate plans in surplus de-risk by extending duration and shifting from return-seeking assets into long credit. Aggregate funded status has sat above one hundred percent for eight consecutive quarters.

**This is the load-bearing assumption of the note.** The demand exists because the funding rules make a surplus worth protecting: contribution requirements and the discount-rate basis for measuring liabilities together give a sponsor a reason to lock in. If the funding rules change such that sponsors can release or re-risk a surplus, the flow at L.1 does not merely slow — it can reverse, and the overweight does not survive that.

## L.3 Supply

Net long-dated corporate issuance has run below the demand described at L.2 for six quarters. The desk does not expect issuers to close the gap while front-end funding remains cheaper.

## L.4 Credit Quality

The desk prefers single-A and better at the long end. This is a flow call, not a credit call, and the desk is not compensated for reaching down in quality to express it.

## L.5 Relationship to the Broader Credit View

This is a long-end specific overweight and does not contradict the index-level underweight at G.1 of FI-US-IG-SPREADS 2025-09. That note is a valuation view on the index; this one is a flow view on a maturity bucket.

## L.6 Risks to the View

A change to pension funding rules that permits surplus release, changes the discount-rate basis, or reduces contribution requirements. This is the risk that would invalidate L.2 and with it the recommendation.

A sharp increase in long-dated issuance. A widening in spreads large enough to make the flow price-insensitive.

## L.7 Review Trigger

Reviewed on the earlier of its twelve-month anniversary or any announced change to pension funding or discount-rate rules.
