# US Duration and Curve Positioning (Note FI-US-DURATION-PATH, Edition 2026-01)

Published 2026-01-15 by US Fixed Income Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2026-02-01.

## D.1 Summary View

The desk recommends a modest long duration position of plus zero point four years against benchmark, expressed in the five to ten year part of the curve, and recommends against expressing duration at the long end.

## D.2 Policy Path

The desk's central case follows the guidance in the Federal Reserve's policy statement of 2026-01, which describes a measured pace of reduction contingent on realised disinflation. See FED Statement 2026-01 at F.2.

The desk assumes three reductions over the coming twelve months against a market-implied two and a half. The half-step difference is the whole of the duration recommendation and should be understood as a modest deviation, not a conviction call.

## D.3 Why Not the Long End

Term premium has re-established itself at a level the desk regards as fair to cheap, but supply at the long end remains heavy and the desk sees no catalyst for compression inside twelve months. A long-end duration position would express a term premium view the desk does not hold.

## D.4 Curve

The desk expects continued steepening between two and ten years and recommends expressing the duration position with a steepening bias rather than as a parallel extension.

## D.5 Risks

An inflation print sequence that removes the contingency in F.2 of the Federal Reserve statement. A fiscal event that increases long-end supply beyond current projections. A disorderly repricing of term premium, which would hurt the position at D.1 despite its front-loaded expression.
