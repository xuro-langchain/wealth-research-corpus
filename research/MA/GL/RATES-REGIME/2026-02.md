# Global Rates Regime and Cross-Asset Implications (Note MA-GL-RATES-REGIME, Edition 2026-02)

Published 2026-02-05 by Multi-Asset Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2026-03-01. This note takes a global view.

## R.1 Summary View

The desk's working assumption is that the policy rate regime has moved from the post-2010 pattern of persistently negative real short rates to one in which real short rates hold in a zero to one and a half percent band through the cycle.

## R.2 What Follows From the Regime

A higher real short rate reduces the equilibrium equity risk premium required to hold duration-like equities, and increases the attraction of fixed income relative to the post-2010 experience. Both effects argue for a higher strategic fixed income weight than the bands in force through 2023.

## R.3 Stock-Bond Correlation

The correlation between equity and duration returns has been unstable and has spent extended periods positive. The desk does not assume a return to reliably negative correlation and recommends that the diversification benefit of duration be underwritten at a materially lower level than the post-2010 sample implies.

## R.4 Implications for the Bands

The desk recommends the global multi-asset bands be reviewed against R.2 and R.3 rather than carried forward. This note does not itself set bands, which are a committee matter.

## R.5 Risks

A return to the post-2010 regime through a demand shock, which would invalidate R.1 directly. A fiscal dominance outcome in which nominal rates are held below inflation deliberately, which would also invalidate R.1 but with the opposite asset implications.
