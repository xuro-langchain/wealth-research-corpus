# Private Markets Allocation Framework (Note MA-GL-PRIVATE-MARKETS, Edition 2025-12)

Published 2025-12-10 by Multi-Asset Research. Applies to positions taken on or after 2026-01-01. This note takes a global view.

## V.1 Summary View

The desk supports a strategic private markets allocation of ten to twenty percent for eligible clients with a genuine ten-year horizon, weighted toward secondaries and private credit and away from primary buyout at current entry multiples.

## V.2 Eligibility Is a Constraint, Not a View

Eligibility for these strategies is determined by the qualified client and accredited investor thresholds, which are a regulatory matter and not a research matter. The desk's view applies only to clients who are eligible; it does not bear on who is eligible. See SEC Order IA-6961 at Q.2 and the firm's eligibility guidance.

## V.3 Secondaries

Discounts to net asset value in the secondary market have narrowed but remain wide of the ten-year median for buyout portfolios. The desk regards this as the most attractive entry point in the private markets complex.

## V.4 Private Credit

Spreads over broadly syndicated loans remain adequate compensation for illiquidity in the desk's view. The desk is watching covenant quality, which has deteriorated at the larger end of the market where the asset class now competes directly with syndicated issuance.

## V.5 Primary Buyout

Entry multiples remain above the desk's estimate of fair value, and the exit environment has not normalised. The desk recommends underweighting primary buyout within any private markets allocation rather than avoiding the asset class.

## V.6 Liquidity

A private markets allocation must be underwritten against the client's spending requirement, not against the allocation's expected return. The desk recommends that no client hold a private markets allocation whose unfunded commitments exceed two years of liquid portfolio spending.

## V.7 Risks

A prolonged closure of the exit environment, which extends duration in an asset class that cannot be rebalanced. Denominator effects in a drawdown, which mechanically breach an allocation band that cannot be traded back.
